Abstract

Pretraining has become a dominant paradigm in learning robotic manipulation policies: models are first pretrained on broad datasets and then fine-tuned on downstream tasks with limited task-specific data. While common practice in pretraining favors large, flat end-to-end policies trained on broad real-world datasets with diverse tasks, we propose an alternative approach. We show that effective manipulation pretraining can be achieved through using a structured hierarchical policy representation, paired with a compact set of core manipulation skills procedurally generated in simulation. The hierarchical policy consists of a high-level policy that predicts subgoal end-effector poses, and a low-level policy that predicts end-effector movements conditioned on these subgoals. We find that the task-relevant goal-following behavior learned by the low-level policy provides a transferable prior for downstream fine-tuning. We further show that pretraining with this hierarchical representation on a set of core skills such as grasping, placing, and articulated-object interaction can produce a strong manipulation prior, while allowing demonstrations to be generated efficiently in simulation without costly real-world data collection. We term our system CHiP, Core-skill Hierarchical Pretraining. Experiments in simulation and the real world show that CHiP achieves stronger downstream fine-tuning performance than flat policy architectures, and that simulation-generated core-skill pretraining performs competitively with DROID pretraining under the same hierarchical policy representation.

CHiP Overview


Overview of CHiP: Core-skill Hierarchical Pretraining. We propose a new perspective for manipulation policy pretraining. For policy representation, we propose to use a hierarchical policy representation instead of a flat end-to-end policy; for pretraining tasks, we propose to pretrain on a compact set of core manipulation skills. We show that these decisions enable CHiP to not only achieve strong zero-shot performance on the pretraining tasks, but also enable effective downstream fine-tuning on new tasks and robot embodiments that are not seen during pretraining.

Real-world Fine-tuning Tasks


We fine-tune the CHiP model, trained on the simulation generated core skills with a 7DoF Franka Arm, on the following 2 long-horizon tasks using a 6DoF Aloha ViperX Arm: Folding an onesie, and Table Bussing. We use 33 fine-tuning demonstrations for onesie folding, and 23 fine-tuning demonstrations for table bussing, to test if CHiP supports sample-efficient fine-tuning.
